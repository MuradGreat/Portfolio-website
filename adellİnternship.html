Day 2 - PLC Quality Testing

On the second day of the internship, each member of student internship team was assigned to specific section of the factory. Şaban Yılmaz assigned me to the Cemal Ergen’s laboratory, where I was introduced to the Delta PLC automation system used to test the quality of the manufactured faucets and detect the presence of any small cracks or holes. The testing process began with firstly putting the faucet with 2 apertures into the testing fixture, where two tubes were connected to. The pneumatic system supplied compressed air into the faucet, thereby increasing the pressure. According to the applicable standards, the pressure was set to 3 bar at the outlet of the faucet, and 6 bars at the inlet. If the pressure was observed to drop during the test, then the faucet was considered defective and was rejected for disposal. Totally, during the internship day about 5 faucets were tested personally. During the second part of the day, the team was assigned to collecting the manufactured pipes into bundles. The working principle of a hand pallet truct was introduced, which later facilitated the process of bundles’ transportations. A picture of the bundled pipes is presented.

Day 3-8 - Core production

The next three days, the team was introduced to the initial stages of faucet manufacturing. The first process presented was the production of the sand core, which is later used in the casting process. Initially, five buckets of sand are poured into a sand hopper, from which the sand is discharged into a sand mixer. During the process, each grain gets coated with a microscopic film of resin. Once cured, that thin film bonds millions of grains together. The primary purpose of the hardener is to chemically react with the resin, creating a rigid three-dimensional polymer network. The mixing process must be carefully controlled. If the mixing time is short, the resin and hardener may not react completely, resulting in a non-uniform mixture. Conversely, excessive mixing can cause the mixture to begin curing prematurely, reducing its usability. As soon as the resin and hardener come into contact, a chemical reaction begins. During the reaction, small resin molecules join together, these molecules form long polymer chains, and those chains become interconnected into a rigid three-dimensional network. This solid polymer binds the sand grains together. After the mixing process is completed, the prepared mixture is used to produce the sand core. Engineering Insight

Increasing the resin ratio above the optimum does not significantly improve the strength of the core. Instead, it increases production cost, reduces gas permeability, generates more smoke during casting, and makes the core difficult to remove after solidification.

After the sand mixture is prepared, it is transported in buckets to the core-making machines. The mixture is then blown through tubes into a heated core box maintained at appxoimately 200 degrees Celsius, where it undergoes curing and takes the required shape. Around 200 degrees is high enough to speed up the chemical reaction between the resin and hardener; fully cure the resin, increasing the mechanical strength of the sand core, and improve the core's strength so it can withstand handling and molten metal. Compressed air is used to blow the sand mixture into the metal core box. The core box has narrow passages and complex cavities. Air pressure packs the sand uniformly and completely fills the mold. Once the curing process is complete, the finished sand core is removed from the mold. After the cores are produced, excess material (flash) remaining on their surfaces is removed manually using a trimming knife to ensure the required dimensions and surface quality.Flash is removed to ensure that the core fits accurately inside the mold cavity. Excess flash may reduce dimensional accuracy, cause core misalignment, or produce unwanted fins on the final casting . During the internship, I was assigned to manually remove the flash from approximately 600 sand cores using a trimming knife.

The mold is then tightly clamped by the casting machine to prevent molten metal from leaking during the pouring process. Initially, brass ingots are placed in a furnace heated by natural gas, which raises the temperature to 1020 degrees Celsius, which is sufficient to completely melt the brass alloy used in production.. Next, with the pressure supplied, compressed air starts transporting brass along through heat-resistant tubes to the pouring nozzle where through a hole brass is poured from the chamber into the mold cavity of the machine. The pouring process lasts approximately 11 seconds. This duration has been determined experimentally to ensure complete filling of the mold while minimizing material waste and preventing casting defects. After the mold cavity is completely filled, the molten brass is allowed to solidify before the casting is removed. After casting, the grippers holding the cast component at approximately 1000 degrees are immersed in a water-cooling tank to facilitate the next stage of the process. The casting is not cooled immediately because it must solidify under controlled conditions inside the mold. Finally, the casting is released onto the carriage.

Day 9-13 - Compressor

On day 6, Intern Supervisor introduced the team to the compressor section of the factory. The company was assigned to manufacture a new product using a new machine. Firsly, a small cylindrical part made of brass was heated up to 800 Celcius degrees. Under careful control, the part was carried from a heating machine directly to the compressor, which initially was also heated by coal for better metal leakage. After placing the detail, the machine was extra lubricated for preventing unexpected outcome, and the part was compressed. After validating the results, the work team determined the tolerance of the product. After making the decision, a second machine was connected. İnitially, the machine is loaded with brass cylinders after which it is switched on, and the parts are automatically carried out to the upper sections for heating. After heating, the parts are sent to the compressor, where above-mentioned process occurs. The machine is electrically driven. The bottleneck of the machine is estimated to be 15,000 pieces per day.

Day 13-15 - Polishing machine

Later the team was introduced the polishing machine. Firsly, a rectangular part made of steel 2367 was given, and a task was assigned to polish its surface. By securing the part on the magnetic surface, the machine was turned On and its instructions were prescribed. By carefully adjusting the altitude and proper flow rate of cooling oil, the team succesed in polishing metal under careful observation of the intern.

Next, the part was mounted on the work table, and a miling machine was switched on. During the machining process, the spindle was continuously supplied with coolant to ensure proper lubrication and heat dissipation. The angular speed was determined to be 3000 RPM: the option was chosen for reliability, prior experience, effective time-management, and machine capabilities. Once the machining process ws complete, the work table was cleaned from burr using air blower.

Day 13-14 - PLC-based cutting machine

The working day was dedicated mainly on transferring tubes from a container and sorting them onto a rotating fixture. A robotics hand then picks up each tube and accurately positions it inside the cutting machine. The machine is capable of processing up to five pieces simultaneously. Once the cutting process is complete, the robotic arm places the finished part on a sliding conveyor, where it moves into a collecion box before being transported to the next stage of the manufacturing process. Lastly, the part is checked with a tool called "Mastar". İf its black end enters a tube smoothly, the part is manufactured correctly. However, if the red one takes over, then the part is considered defective.

Day 15-17 - Polishing tube internals

The day was dedicated to washing and polishing the inner surface of tubes. Each tube is positioned inside the machine, securely clamped using two pneumatic metal fixtures. Once the machine was started, the part is cut according to the pre-programmed CNC code. A video demonstrating the operation of the machine is available in the Gallery section.
